# User Guide: Getting Started with House Financial Advisor

**Welcome!** This guide is written for you, a household member who wants to understand your finances better. No technical knowledge required.

---

## What is House Financial Advisor?

Think of House Financial Advisor as your **personal financial assistant**—one that lives on your home computer, not in the cloud. It helps you:

- **See your money** at a glance (checking, savings, credit cards, loans)
- **Budget together** (set spending limits and track progress)
- **Reach your goals** (save for vacation, pay off debt, emergency fund)
- **Understand trends** (spending patterns, cash flow, financial health)
- **Ask questions** (conversational AI advisor that explains recommendations)
- **Make decisions** (evidence-based guidance you can approve or reject)

**Key difference**: Your financial data stays in your home, not on someone else's server.

## Getting Started (15 minutes)

### Step 1: Your Household Profile (2 minutes)

When you first open House Financial Advisor, you'll set up your household:

1. **Household name**: "Smith Family" or just your name
2. **Household type**: Couple, single, or family
3. **Add members**: You and anyone else managing finances
4. **Financial goals**: What matters most? (Debt payoff, savings, investment, etc.)

**That's it!** You've established your household profile.

### Step 2: Add Your Accounts (5 minutes)

Next, you'll add your financial accounts:

1. **Checking account** (your main spending account)
2. **Savings account** (emergency fund or general savings)
3. **Credit cards** (all cards you use)
4. **Loans** (mortgage, car loan, student loans)
5. **Investments** (401k, IRA, brokerage, etc.)

For each account, just enter:
- Where it is (bank name: "Wells Fargo", "Chase", etc.)
- What type (checking, savings, credit card, etc.)
- Current balance
- Interest rate (if applicable)

**Example**: 
- "Wells Fargo Checking" - $2,104
- "Capital One Credit Card" - $12,543 (owed)
- "Chase Savings" - $8,000

### Step 3: Budget Setup (5 minutes)

The system will suggest budget categories based on typical households:

- **Housing** (rent/mortgage, property tax, insurance)
- **Food** (groceries, dining out)
- **Utilities** (electricity, water, internet)
- **Transportation** (car payment, gas, insurance, public transit)
- **Healthcare** (insurance, copays, medications)
- **Entertainment** (hobbies, streaming, dining)
- **Savings** (emergency fund, goals)
- **Debt** (minimum payments on credit cards/loans)
- **Other** (everything else)

You'll estimate monthly spending for each category. The system will help you based on what it learns from your accounts.

### You're Done! 

Congratulations! You now have a complete picture of your household finances.

## Importing Your Statements (Monthly)

To keep your finances up-to-date, you'll import your bank and credit card statements monthly:

### Import from Your Bank

Most banks let you download statements as CSV (Comma Separated Values—basically a spreadsheet):

1. **Log into your bank** (Wells Fargo, Chase, etc.)
2. **Download statement as CSV** (usually under "Statements" or "Export")
3. **Open House Financial Advisor**
4. **Click "Upload Statement"**
5. **Select the CSV file**
6. **Review transactions** the system extracted
7. **Approve** (one-click approval for familiar accounts)

**That's it!** The system automatically:
- Reads all transactions
- Matches them with your budget categories
- Detects recurring charges (utilities, subscriptions)
- Updates your account balance
- Recalculates everything

### What If Something Looks Wrong?

If a transaction seems wrong:

1. Click the transaction
2. Correct it (change amount, category, date)
3. Save
4. The system updates automatically

**Example**: 
- Grocery store might be miscategorized as "Food" → Correct to "Groceries"
- $50 Amazon payment might be tagged wrong → Correct to "Entertainment"

## Asking Your AI Advisor Questions (Anytime)

Once your finances are set up, you can ask questions in natural language:

### Budget Questions

**You ask**: "Why are we over budget on dining out?"

**Advisor responds**:
- Shows your spending vs. budget
- Identifies the top 3 restaurants you visited
- Suggests ways to reduce (cook more, find cheaper options)
- Asks if you want to adjust the budget instead

### Spending Questions

**You ask**: "Should we prioritize paying off credit card or building emergency fund?"

**Advisor responds**:
- Analyzes both scenarios (payoff vs. savings)
- Calculates impact (interest saved, timeline)
- Recommends balanced approach (split payments)
- Shows evidence and alternatives
- You can approve, modify, or reject recommendation

### Goal Questions

**You ask**: "How much should we save per month for vacation?"

**Advisor responds**:
- Checks current savings and budget
- Calculates monthly savings needed (for your target)
- Shows impact on other goals
- Suggests timeline
- Updates the system when you approve

### Unexpected Money Questions

**You ask**: "We got a $5K tax refund. What should we do?"

**Advisor responds**:
- Models three scenarios (all to credit card, all to emergency fund, split)
- Shows 1-year, 5-year impact on net worth
- Recommends based on your financial goals
- You can approve one scenario
- System automatically adjusts budget and goals

### Curiosity Questions

**You ask**: "Are we doing better than average?"

**Advisor responds**:
- Shows your metrics (savings rate, debt-to-income, etc.)
- Compares to typical households (anonymized)
- Identifies strengths (good emergency fund)
- Suggests improvements (lower credit card debt)

## Privacy: Your Data Stays Private

**You might ask**: "Where does my financial data go?"

**The answer**: Nowhere outside your home (by default).

- ✅ Your data stays on your computer/server
- ✅ No cloud backup unless you explicitly enable it
- ✅ No selling your data to third parties
- ✅ No data brokers have access
- ✅ When AI advisor helps, your data is anonymized (no names, account numbers)

**Example**: 
- Your data: "John Smith, Wells Fargo account 9876543210, SSN 123-45-6789, $12,543 credit card debt"
- What AI sees: "Household with $12,543 credit card debt at ~19% APR"
- What AI cannot see: Your name, account number, SSN

If you want backups, you control them (your NAS, your external drive, your cloud).

## Common Questions

### Q: Do I have to use the AI advisor?

A: No. The dashboard and budget tools work perfectly without it. The AI advisor is optional—use it when you want advice, ignore it when you don't.

### Q: What if I don't like a recommendation?

A: You don't have to approve it. Recommendations don't change your finances unless you explicitly approve them.

### Q: How often do I need to update my data?

A: Monthly is typical (import statements once/month). Weekly is fine if you're budget-conscious. Daily is overkill.

## Troubleshooting

### "I'm having trouble importing a statement"

**Try this**:
1. Make sure the file is CSV format (not Excel, not PDF)
2. Check that the bank is recognized (Wells Fargo, Chase, etc.)
3. Try a shorter statement (one week instead of one month)
4. If still stuck, email support@advisor.local with the file

## Tips for Success

### Tip 1: Set it up once, maintain it monthly

Spend 15 minutes setting up initially. Then 15 minutes/month importing statements. That's it!

### Tip 2: Make it a household conversation

Talk about the budget together. "Why are we over on dining?" "Should we adjust the goal?" Finances should be transparent and collaborative.

### Tip 3: Use the advisor for big decisions

Don't ask about every $5 coffee. Do ask: "Should we pay off the credit card or start an emergency fund?" Those are decisions where the advisor adds real value.

### Tip 4: Check monthly, not obsessively

Weekly checking is fine, but daily checking usually just causes stress. Monthly review is perfect for most households.

### Tip 5: Be honest with your numbers

The system is only as good as your data. Be accurate with amounts and categories, and you'll get better insights.

## Glossary of Terms (Just in case)

**Account**: A place you hold money (checking, savings, credit card, loan, investment)

**Balance**: How much money is in an account (positive for assets, negative for debt)

**Budget**: A spending plan for each category (housing, food, utilities, etc.)

**Cash flow**: Money flowing in (income) and out (spending) each month

**Credit card debt**: Money you owe on credit cards (usually high interest)

**CSV**: A file format for data (stands for "Comma Separated Values")

**Debt-to-income ratio**: Your total debt divided by your monthly income (lower is better)

**Emergency fund**: Money saved for unexpected expenses (car repair, medical, etc.)

**Net worth**: Your total assets minus total debt (the "big picture" number)

**Recurring charge**: Something you pay for every month (utilities, subscriptions)

**Savings rate**: The percentage of income you save (higher is better)

**Surplus**: Money left over after bills (income minus expenses)
